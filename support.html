Support

For BiblioHarvest 2.9.x on Windows 10 and 11 · Replies within two business days

Most questions are answered below. If yours is not, write to support.biblioharvest@gmail.com with the app version (Settings → About) and what you were doing when the problem appeared.

Getting started

How do I run my first search?
Install from the Microsoft Store, open the app and type your research question in the box on the home screen. Leave the query options at their defaults for a first run — the app expands the question into keyword variations for you. Choose the databases, press Search, and results appear in the table as each source returns.

Do I need an API key to use it?
No. Searching, screening, curation and export work with the built-in local relevance engine and no key of any kind. Keys are only needed if you want the optional AI features.

Which databases are searched?
PubMed, Europe PMC, OpenAlex, Crossref and arXiv. You can limit a run to a subset.

Does it work offline?
Screening an existing result set and exporting workbooks work offline. New searches, PDF retrieval and AI calls need an internet connection.

The free allowance and credits

How much can I use for free?
Every install gets a daily allowance of use at no cost, reset each day. The remaining time is shown in the status bar and in Settings → Usage, which is the authoritative figure for your machine.

What happens when the allowance runs out?
A dialog offers three choices: wait for the next day's allowance, buy a credit pack from the Microsoft Store, or complete an optional sponsored view for a few extra minutes. Nothing is deleted — your results and curated set are still there when you come back.

Do credits expire?
No. Purchased credits stay on the machine until they are used.

How do I get a refund?
Purchases are processed by Microsoft, so refunds go through Microsoft Store support, not the developer.

Why is there a sponsored line?
It funds the free tier. It is one dismissible line above the status bar plus an optional sponsored view; closing it hides it for the session, and it never covers your work. The current build shows the developer's own messages only — no third-party ad network and no data collected for advertising. See the privacy policy.

What is free, and what needs a sponsored view?
Search, screening, curation and export are free. The free plan includes three free uses each of AI with your own key, PDF download and analysis. After those, and always for a database search and the initial Excel export, one short sponsored view unlocks one use — five unlocks a day, resetting at midnight. Declining is always allowed and nothing is permanently locked.

How much is Pro?
₹50 for one week or ₹250 for one month, with every limit and all advertising removed. Subscriptions are not sold yet; the app says so when you open its plans window.

AI features

Which providers are supported?
Google Gemini, OpenAI, Groq, and Ollama for a model running on your own machine. Enter the key (or the local endpoint, for Ollama) in Settings → AI.

Where does my key go?
It is stored locally in %LOCALAPPDATA%\BiblioHarvest and sent only to the provider you selected. It is never transmitted to the developer.

The AI button says it is unavailable.
That means no provider is configured, or the key was rejected. Check the key, the provider's quota and your connection. Everything else in the app continues to work without it.

Should I trust the AI output?
Treat it as a first pass. It can be incomplete or wrong, and it is not a substitute for reading the paper. Verify anything you put in a thesis or a submission.

Data and privacy

Where is my work stored?
In %LOCALAPPDATA%\BiblioHarvest, together with exported workbooks wherever you saved them. Copy that folder to back up your settings and curated sets.

Can you see my searches?
No. There is no BiblioHarvest account and no developer server. The only outbound traffic is to the sources described in the privacy policy.

How do I remove everything?
Uninstall from Windows Settings, then delete %LOCALAPPDATA%\BiblioHarvest.

Troubleshooting

A search returns nothing.
Check that at least one database is selected, and try a broader question. Some fields and filters are not supported by every source; the app drops the unsupported part of the query rather than failing the whole run.

PDFs will not download.
Only open-access copies are fetched automatically. Many publisher pages need a click-through or an institutional login, so the app hands those to your browser. A paywalled article will not download — use your library's access.

A journal metric is blank.
Deliberate. Values are only filled where a public source provides them for that title and year; the app leaves the cell empty rather than guessing.

The app will not start after an update.
In Windows Settings → Apps, use Advanced options → Repair for BiblioHarvest, then Reset if repair does not help. Reset removes local settings, so copy the data folder first if you keep curated sets there.

Reporting a bug.
Send the app version, what you did, what happened, and the most recent file from the app's logs folder. Remove any API keys before attaching a log or screenshot.
